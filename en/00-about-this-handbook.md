# **About this handbook**

This handbook explains how to work with [bun.ink](http://bun.ink): writing, managing versions, working on a text together with others. It's written for people who write professionally or as a hobby — not for people who code.

## Who it's for

You don't need any prior knowledge of Git. If you already know what a commit is, feel free to skip the explanations; if you don't, the handbook will get you there as you work through the chapters in order. Every term is explained exactly once, at the point where you first need it.

What you do need: a browser and a GitHub account. That's enough.

## How it's organised

Chapters 01 to 03 get you writing — account, editor, and how projects, folders and documents fit together.

Chapters 04 to 07 are the real heart of it: what happens when your texts live in a repository? Connecting a repository, then branches as versions, then commits and history, and finally pull requests for working with your editor. These four build on one another, so read them in order.

From chapter 08 onwards it's reference material rather than reading: AI agents, encrypted areas, statistics and export. These chapters stand on their own.

The full overview is in the repository's table of contents.

## Why the handbook lives in a repository

Because it demonstrates exactly what it describes.

This handbook is written in bun.ink. Every chapter is written in the editor and saved straight into this repository. Drafts live on branches. Revisions come back as pull requests — some from a person, some from an AI agent that reads the repository and suggests changes.

So you can look at the history and trace how this text was worked on: which version came about when, what the editor changed, what was adopted and what wasn't. That's precisely the difference from a text that sits in a document whose backstory nobody remembers.

## Languages

Writing happens in German. The English version is generated from it and not edited by hand. If you spot a mistake in the English version, it's almost always already there in the German text.

## Found a mistake?

Then report it as an issue in this repository, or send a pull request straight to the German version. Both help — including simply pointing out that a passage is hard to follow, even if you don't know how to put it better.

Stuck somewhere in the handbook, or dealing with a bun.ink problem that isn't covered here?

In that case, please get in touch with our support at any time: [info@bun.ink](mailto:info@bun.ink). We'll try to solve your problem as quickly as possible, or suggest ways around it.

Wishes for the app itself — requests for new functions and features, for instance — don't belong here; they go in the public roadmap.
